# How to read these notes

Inkhorse counts its versions two ways. A revision (rev) is every change put on the website; the number goes up by one each time. A release (1.0, 1.1, and so on) is a revision Steve has tested on his iPhone and called stable. Only releases go on the live app.

The newest release is first. Under each release is a short summary of what changed for people using Inkhorse, then the revisions in detail, newest first. A revision marked "preview" was on the preview page for testing and had not yet gone live.

The built-in User manual explains how to use Inkhorse as it is today. These notes are the history.

# Release 1.2 · in progress

## What's new for you

- Book-style punctuation: when you read or export, two hyphens (--) show as a dash with no spaces (—), three dots (...) as one ellipsis (…), and a range of years like 1980-1981 as 1980–1981. A line never starts with a dash. What you typed is not changed.
- Justify now means justified on a phone too, with long words hyphenated to keep the spacing even. Before, a phone quietly showed a ragged edge even with Justify on. Uncheck Justify in Aa if you prefer the ragged edge.
- Scene breaks (* * *) in the EPUB, Word file and print PDF are now a centered ornament, and the paragraph after one has no indent, the same as on screen.
- Exports of a book with its own title and copyright pages: each page now comes out once, as front matter, and stays out of the Contents (before, both came out twice).
- Clear all, at the top of the Highlights list in the Book Hub: tap it twice to clear every highlight in the book.
- Next mark, in Write: a lime pencil button with the number of marks left now sits at the right end of the page bar at the top, so you keep tapping it, one tap per mark, with the keyboard up and no trip back to Tools. After the last mark it says "That was the last mark"; the next tap goes back to the first. While you have marks on a phone, the "of N" beside the page number (and, on narrower phones, the word count) steps aside to make room.
- Read aloud on a computer: with Chrome's Google voices (like Google UK English Male), the highlight now keeps up with the voice. Those voices are made on the internet and their word signals arrive late, so Inkhorse follows the sound by time instead and learns the voice's pace over the first few sentences.
- On a computer, in your own book: the text cursor shows over the words, and a click on a word opens the page for fixing right there. Tips speak to a mouse and keyboard on a computer and to a finger on a phone or tablet.
- Computer keys in Read: Space plays or pauses reading aloud (full screen too), F goes in and out of full screen, Esc backs out one step (a panel, then full screen), Home and End go to the start and end of the chapter, and F11 also hides Inkhorse's controls.
- On a computer, Fit my screen (the first choice in the size menu, which was called This screen) fills the whole window with the open book.
- Read aloud: if Meilin, Calvin or Soren drops a few words from a sentence (like a name), Inkhorse notices and has her read it again in smaller pieces, so nothing is skipped. ↺ also makes a sentence fresh.
- Backup files now end in .inkhorse, so the iPhone's Files app no longer tries to open them (a big backup could freeze it on Loading…). Older .json backups still restore.
- Chapter opening, in each book's settings (Chapters panel, under Author): choose Classic, Ornament, Big number or Drop cap for how every chapter's first page looks, in Read, the print PDF and the EPUB.
- Chapter openings: the label (like CHAPTER ONE) now sits just above the chapter title, a third of the way down the page, with nothing at the top, the way professionally formatted books open a chapter. The same in Read, the print PDF and the EPUB.
- Book Hub: the page counter below the book is now a button marked Book Hub. Tap it to go to any page, slide through the book, see and copy your highlights, and open any chapter.
- Copy, beside Clear page when the Highlighter is on: copies the highlights on the page you're looking at, with their paragraph breaks, ready to paste into Write, Notes or another app.
- Show tips, in the Aa panel: turn off the dark notes that teach you the app once you know your way around. Messages about what's happening now, like Getting ready… and Saved, still show.
- The book fills the space it has on a phone or iPad, held upright or sideways. Held upright, you see one big page even with Two-page spread chosen; turn it sideways and the open book comes back. A cover on its own sits in the middle of the screen.
- In full screen, a tap on the left or right side turns the page straight away, one page per tap, in order, however fast you tap. Double-tap the middle of the page to play or pause reading aloud.

Rev 232 (November 10, 2026, evening, preview): Steve's Blender book, brought in from its PDF, read wrong from the first pages, and his screenshots showed why. The "Contents at a Glance" was printed under the PDF's Copyright Page bookmark, so it was swallowed into the copyright page as run-together paragraphs. Because that page was then long, it stopped counting as a front page, so it became Chapter 1, the Table of Contents became Chapter 2, and every number after that was off: the counter read "Ch 5 · Chapter 1: Discovering Blender". Now: a page named Copyright Page, Contents, Table of Contents or Contents at a Glance is a front page however long it is; a Contents at a Glance becomes its own front page and is shown one entry per line; when a book numbers its own chapters, Inkhorse uses the book's numbers and gives none of its own to the Introduction, Part pages or Index; a chapter's "Chapter 1" and title lines are no longer repeated as text under its heading; a section such as "Discovering Blender's origins…" is no longer taken for the chapter "Discovering Blender"; and a word too long for its contents line breaks at the margin instead of running off the page. Then Steve sent his actual PDF, and the rest was found and fixed with it. Words run together ("IconsUsedinThisBook", "Themenuthatisapie"): the PDF reader Inkhorse uses (pdf.js) adds a font's word spacing to every space even in two-byte fonts, where the PDF rules say it must not, and in this Ghostscript-made PDF that gave the spaces a negative width, so they vanished; Inkhorse now loads the reader with that one line corrected (75 run-together words in the book before, 1 after, and that one is the book's own). Running heads ("CHAPTER 1 Discovering Blender 11", "12 PART 1 Wrapping Your Brain Around Blender") are left out of the text. A drop capital printed apart from its word ("I" over "n the world of 3D…") is joined back. The Introduction's own sections (About This Book, Where to Go from Here) stay inside the Introduction instead of becoming chapters. Bookmarks written "Chapter 1 Discovering Blender", with no colon, are read as the book's own numbered chapters. On the real book: 0 contents lines past the margin, and 60 page turns from Chapter 1 each added exactly one. Steve's file was used only for testing on this machine; it is not in the repo. Rev 231 (November 10, 2026, evening, preview): Page numbers that hold still. Steve, reading a 600-page book he had added: "I'd be on page 6, and the next thing you know, I'm in chapter 12." Two causes were found and fixed. First, in a big book the page number was a guess that Inkhorse corrected one chapter at a time; on a 700-page test book the number under the page changed 28 times in two seconds (~291, ~340, ~329 ...). Inkhorse now never shows a guessed page number: it says "Counting pages…", the foot of the page stays blank, and then the exact number appears and stays; every turn adds one. Counting is also quicker, and a text size you have used before comes back at once without recounting. Second, on a Contents page the page number at the right-hand end of every line was a link, sitting exactly where a thumb taps to turn the page, so a tap meant "next page" could throw the reader chapters ahead (reproduced in the manual: page 5 to page 15). Now only the words of an entry are a link, away from the page edges, and an entry that names a chapter opens that chapter directly. Go to page, the Fix bar and the read-aloud bar also use exact numbers only. Rules touched: F7.4 (whole-book page numbers) and F6.3 (contents entries are links). Two new automatic tests. Found while testing and NOT fixed yet: a "For Dummies"-style PDF's two contents pages come in as run-together paragraphs, and such a book's Introduction is labelled "Ch 1" beside the book's own "Chapter 1". Waiting for Steve to approve. Not tested: Steve's iPhone, or his actual book.

## What's new for you

- Version line: Inkhorse shows its version at the bottom of the Chapters panel and when you tap the save dot. The manual was fixed to match the app exactly.
- Your own web address: Inkhorse runs at getinkhorse.com and keeps your book in your browser instead of a Claude account.
- Colors: Light, StyleKnight (the signature dark theme), Sepia, Sky, and your own page and text colors, under Aa.
- At night: a second theme on a schedule, 8:00 PM to 7:00 AM unless you change it, and Night warmth for a warmer screen with less blue light.
- Hoofprint: Inkhorse learns your names, places and brands, offers a fix when dictation mishears them, and remembers every fix you make.
- Covers: the front cover sits on its own like a closed book, turns like a page, and is always shown whole.
- Book-shaped pages on phones, tablets and computers, instead of stretched ones.
- Sections flow down the same page, like a printed memoir, with a check box to give each section its own page.
- Curly quotes when you read and export. What you typed is not changed.
- Contents with page numbers in the manual.
- Print PDF: the cover is left out of the print file and the type is built into the file, as printers ask.
- Imports: PDF paragraphs come in whole, and a contents page that arrives as one long block is put one entry per line.

# How these notes are kept

Every revision gets a line here, in plain words, the same day it is made. When Steve calls a revision stable, its release gets its "What's new for you" summary and the date. The User manual never carries this history; it only explains how to use Inkhorse as it is now.

The master copy is CHANGELOG.md in the Inkhorse code folder. The e-book, Inkhorse Release Notes, is built from it.
