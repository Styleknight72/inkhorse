# Why this standard exists

Inkhorse opens books from many places: EPUB e-books, PDFs, Word files, plain text, and books written right inside the app. Each of those arrives formatted its own way. A reader should never be able to tell where a book came from. Every book in Inkhorse should look like it was set by the same careful publisher.

This book is that publisher's rulebook. It is the yardstick for every formatting change to Inkhorse: a change either moves Inkhorse closer to this standard or it doesn't go in.

## The benchmark

Our target is "Vellum quality." Vellum is a book-formatting app widely regarded as the gold standard for self-published books, and its pages look like the pages of a book from a traditional publisher. The rules here come from four places:

1. The Chicago Manual of Style, the rulebook most American book publishers follow.
2. Books from traditional publishers, which set the look readers expect without thinking about it.
3. Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP) and Apple Books, which publish the technical rules a book must meet to be sold.
4. The habits of good typography that have held steady for decades.

## How to read the rules

Every rule has a number, like F3.2. When Claude changes Inkhorse, the change names the rules it touches, and the automatic test checks them. When you find a page that looks wrong, the rule number tells us exactly what broke.

Some rules say "In print." Those apply to the print PDF and paperback. Everything else applies to every book on every screen.

# Part 1 · The order of a book

## F1 · Front matter, body, back matter

F1.1 A book runs in this order. Any part can be left out, but the order never changes:

- Cover
- Title page: title, subtitle, author
- Copyright page
- Dedication
- Epigraph: a short opening quote
- Contents
- Foreword or preface
- The chapters
- Afterword, acknowledgments, about the author

F1.2 The cover is a single picture in the standard 2:3 book shape. Every cover in the Library shows at the same 2:3 size, whole, never cut off.

F1.3 The title page carries no page number and no page header.

F1.4 The copyright page sits on the back of the title page in print, set smaller than the body text.

F1.5 Each front-matter page starts on a new page.

# Part 2 · Chapter openings

## F2 · How a chapter begins

F2.1 Every chapter starts on a new page. In print, novels and memoirs often start each chapter on a right-hand page.

F2.2 The chapter title sits about a third of the way down the page, not at the very top. That white space, called the sink, tells the reader a new chapter has begun.

F2.3 A small label such as "CHAPTER 3" may sit above the title, the same in every chapter of the book.

F2.4 The first paragraph of a chapter is not indented. Its first line runs flush to the left margin.

F2.5 The first paragraph may open with a drop cap, a large first letter, or with its first few words in small capitals. Whichever a book uses, it uses for every chapter.

F2.6 No page header on a chapter's first page. The page number may appear at the bottom.

F2.7 Chapter titles are all set the same way in a book: the same font, size, spacing and capitalization.

# Part 3 · Body text

## F3 · Paragraphs

F3.1 Use a book typeface with serifs for the body text. In print, 10 to 12 point.

F3.2 Mark a new paragraph one way only: either an indent on its first line, or a blank line between paragraphs. Never both. Novels and memoirs use the indent with no blank line.

F3.3 The indent is small: about one to one and a half times the size of the type. Amazon's own guideline is 0.2 inches.

F3.4 The first paragraph after a chapter title, a section heading or a scene break is not indented.

F3.5 Lines are justified, meaning both edges are straight, with hyphenation turned on so the spaces between words stay even. A paragraph should never show wide rivers of white space.

F3.6 Line spacing is comfortable and even: about 1.3 to 1.5 times the type size.

F3.7 No widows or orphans. A single line of a paragraph should not sit alone at the top or the bottom of a page.

F3.8 No empty paragraphs. Space between parts of a chapter comes from a scene break (F5), never from blank lines.

F3.9 A line ends only where a paragraph ends. A sentence is never broken into separate lines in the middle.

# Part 4 · Punctuation and type

## F4 · The small marks that make a book look published

F4.1 Quotation marks and apostrophes are curly: “like this” and it’s, not "like this" and it's.

F4.2 A dash that sets off a thought is an em dash with no spaces around it: the bridge—the old one—was gone.

F4.3 A range of numbers uses an en dash: pages 12–19, 1980–1981.

F4.4 An ellipsis is three dots treated as one mark: …

F4.5 One space after a period, never two.

F4.6 Italics for emphasis and for titles of books, films and ships. Never underline, and never use all capitals for emphasis.

F4.7 Every word keeps its spaces. Two words run together, such as "Discoveringparents," are a fault (see F9).

# Part 5 · Headings, breaks and pictures

## F5 · Inside a chapter

F5.1 Section headings inside a chapter are smaller than the chapter title and set the same way every time.

F5.2 A scene break, a change of time or place within a chapter, is marked with a centered ornament, such as three spaced asterisks or a small symbol. A bare blank line is not enough, because it disappears when it falls at the bottom of a page.

F5.3 Pictures are centered, sized to the page width or a set fraction of it, and never stretched or squashed.

F5.4 A caption sits directly under its picture, smaller than the body text.

F5.5 Lists, letters and quoted passages are set off from the body text by indenting both sides, and are the same in every chapter.

# Part 6 · The contents page

## F6 · Contents

F6.1 The contents page is titled "Contents."

F6.2 One entry per line. A long title wraps onto a second line under itself; it never runs into the next entry.

F6.3 Each entry is joined to its page number by a dotted line, and the numbers line up at the right edge.

F6.4 Chapter entries and section entries look different: sections sit indented under their chapter, smaller or lighter.

F6.5 The page numbers are the book's own numbers on the screen it is being read on. Page numbers printed in a paper book belong to the paper book and are left off.

F6.6 Every entry is a link. Tapping a title or its number opens that page.

F6.7 Nothing else on the contents page: no repeated page headers, no leftover print page numbers, no running text.

F6.8 Every contents page in Inkhorse looks the same, whatever file the book came from.

# Part 7 · Page numbers and page headers

## F7 · Numbering

F7.1 In print, front matter is numbered in small Roman numerals (i, ii, iii). Chapter one starts again at 1.

F7.2 Blank pages and chapter-opening pages show no page header.

F7.3 In print, the left-hand page header shows the author's name and the right-hand header shows the book title, or the chapter title. Small capitals or italics, smaller than the body text.

F7.4 On screen, Inkhorse shows its own page numbers for the whole book, counted on the screen being used, with "page X of Y."

# Part 8 · E-books

## F8 · Rules for EPUB and Kindle

F8.1 An e-book reflows: the reader chooses the font and the size, and the pages change. So an e-book has no fixed page numbers, page headers or footers inside its text.

F8.2 The contents page is a working list of links, built from the book's chapter headings. Apple Books and Kindle both build their contents menu from it.

F8.3 Chapters are marked as headings (Heading 1), so readers and stores can find them.

F8.4 No cover picture inside the text. The cover is supplied separately as the cover.

F8.5 Paragraphs are real paragraphs: one return at the end of a paragraph, never at the end of each line, and never tabs or spaces for indents.

F8.6 Footnotes are links that go to the note and back.

F8.7 Pictures are images, not drawn shapes or text boxes.

F8.8 Check the finished e-book in Apple Books and in Kindle Previewer before selling it.

# Part 9 · Print books

## F9 · Rules for the print PDF and paperback

F9.1 Common trim sizes are 5 × 8, 5.5 × 8.5 and 6 × 9 inches. Six by nine is the most common size for nonfiction and memoir; 5 × 8 and 5.5 × 8.5 are common for novels.

F9.2 Amazon's minimum inside margin, the gutter by the spine, depends on the page count:

- 24 to 150 pages: 0.375 inch
- 151 to 300 pages: 0.5 inch
- 301 to 500 pages: 0.625 inch
- 501 to 700 pages: 0.75 inch
- 701 to 828 pages: 0.875 inch

F9.3 Amazon's minimum outside, top and bottom margins are 0.25 inch without bleed and 0.375 inch with bleed. These are minimums. A good-looking book uses more, often 0.5 to 0.75 inch.

F9.4 The print interior file holds no cover. The cover is its own file, sized for the spine.

F9.5 Chapters in print may start on right-hand pages, which can leave a blank left-hand page before them. Blank pages carry no number and no header.

These margin and trim figures were checked against Amazon's help pages on October 28, 2026. Check them again before sending a book to print, since the stores change their rules.

# Part 10 · Books that come in from other files

## F10 · Cleaning up an imported book

Books arrive with leftovers from their old format. Inkhorse removes or repairs them so the book meets this standard. It never changes the author's words, only how they are laid out.

F10.1 Words run together. PDFs often store text without the spaces between words. Inkhorse puts back a space where the letters have a visible gap.

F10.2 Lines broken in the middle of a sentence. A PDF stores each printed line separately. Inkhorse joins them back into whole paragraphs.

F10.3 Leftover print page numbers, page headers and footers are removed.

F10.4 Hyphens that only split a word at the end of a printed line are removed and the word is joined back together.

F10.5 A run-on contents page, with several entries in one paragraph, is split back into one entry per line (F6).

F10.6 Chapters are found from the book's own headings or bookmarks, so every chapter starts on a new page (F2.1).

F10.7 A PDF that is kept as pictures of its pages shows each page exactly as printed. Its text cannot be reformatted. For the full Inkhorse standard, add the book as an EPUB or Word file instead.

# Part 11 · Where Inkhorse stands today

## F11 · Scorecard at rev 91

Meets the standard:

- F1.2 Covers: one 2:3 size, whole cover always shown.
- F3.2 and F3.3 Paragraph indent, with no blank lines between paragraphs.
- F3.4 No indent on the paragraph after a section heading.
- F3.5 Justified text with hyphenation.
- F4.1 Curly quotes and apostrophes when a book is shown or exported.
- F6.6 Tappable contents entries in the manual.
- F7.4 Whole-book page numbers on screen.
- F8.2 and F8.3 EPUB export with a working contents list and chapter headings.
- F9.4 Print PDF without the cover.

Done on the preview page, waiting for Steve's approval (the formatting engine):

- F1.3 and F1.4 Title, copyright and dedication pages recognised as front matter: no chapter label, no paragraph indents, not counted as chapters.
- F2.2 The chapter title about a third of the way down its first page.
- F2.3 The book's own label (PART 1, CHAPTER THREE) shown above the title in place of "Chapter N."
- F2.4 The first paragraph of every chapter not indented.
- F2.5 Every chapter opens with its first line in small capitals.
- F2.7 A chapter with no heading takes its name from the book's own contents list.
- F3.7 No widows or orphans: the whole manual has none (the live app had 24).
- F5.2 Scene breaks set as a centered ornament.
- F6.2 to F6.8 One contents style for every book, whatever its file type.
- F10.1 Spaces put back in PDF text that has none.
- F10.5 Run-on contents pages split into one entry per line.
- F10.6 A label above a chapter title kept with the title.
- The "Import check" pop-up removed.

Not yet:

- F7.1 Roman numerals for front matter in the print PDF.
- F1.3 front-matter detection for Word, PDF and text files (EPUB is done).

Found by loading this very book into Inkhorse (rev 91), now fixed on the preview page:

- F2.3 and F10.6 A small label above a chapter title, such as "PART 1" or "CHAPTER THREE," makes the EPUB importer lose the chapter title. Many professionally made e-books do this. Inkhorse must keep the label and the title together.
- F1.3 and F1.4 The title page and copyright page come in as ordinary indented body text and get "CHAPTER 1" and "CHAPTER 2" labels. Front matter needs its own look and no chapter numbers.
- After importing, a pop-up says "Import check: no dotted lines found." It means nothing to a reader and should not appear.

Still to check against real books:

- F3.9, F10.2, F10.3 and F10.4 on Steve's own imported books: MANtality, Blender for Dummies and the memoir.

# How we keep the standard

1. Every formatting change names the rules it touches.
2. Each rule that can be checked by machine gets an automatic test, like the cover-size and big-book speed tests.
3. Every change goes to the preview page first and goes live only when Steve approves it on his iPhone.
4. The scorecard in Part 11 is updated with every release.
5. This book is updated whenever the standard changes, and its edition line says when. The history of changes to Inkhorse itself is kept in the Inkhorse Release Notes, not in this book or in the User manual.
