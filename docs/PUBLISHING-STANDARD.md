# Why this standard exists

Writing a book and publishing a book are two different jobs. Plenty of finished books never leave the phone they were written on, because the last mile is full of small questions nobody answers in one place. Is it really done? What file do I send? Do I need a number on it? What will the store turn away?

Inkhorse does not publish your book. You do, under your own name, in the stores you choose. What Inkhorse owes you is this: when you carry your book out the door, it is ready. Nothing missing, nothing a store will send back, nothing that makes a first book look like a first book.

This standard says what “ready” means. It sits beside The Inkhorse Formatting Standard. That book is about how every page should look. This one is about what a whole book needs before it meets a reader.

Each rule has a number, like P3.2, so a problem can be traced to the rule that covers it. Part 11 is the scorecard: what Inkhorse does for you today, what is yours to check, and what Inkhorse cannot do yet.

Your book is yours. Nothing in this standard gives Inkhorse any claim on it, and nothing here is legal or tax advice.

# Part 1 · The book is whole

A book is ready when nothing in it is still waiting to be written.

P1.1 The book has a title and an author name, spelled the way they should appear in a store. The name on the cover, the title page and the store listing is the same name.

P1.2 Every chapter has words in it. No chapter is empty, and no chapter is only a note about what will go there.

P1.3 No notes to yourself are left in the text: no “fix this later,” no “check the date,” no row of question marks. Search every chapter for the marks you use, and clear your highlights and their notes before you export.

P1.4 The chapters are in their final order, and each one has the title it will carry in the Contents.

P1.5 Names, dates and places are spelled the same way all the way through. Pick one spelling and search for the others.

# Part 2 · It has been heard

Your eye skips what your ear catches. A book that has only been looked at is not finished.

P2.1 The whole book has been listened to, start to finish, with Read aloud. Not skimmed: heard.

P2.2 Every place where the voice stumbled, ran out of breath or said something you did not mean has been fixed.

P2.3 After the last fix, the changed chapters have been heard once more. A fix can break the sentence next to it.

P2.4 One other person has read it, if you can arrange that. A reader who was not there when it was written sees what the writer cannot.

# Part 3 · The front pages are right

The first pages tell a store, a library and a reader whose book this is.

P3.1 The book opens in this order: cover, title page, copyright page, dedication if there is one, contents, then the first chapter.

P3.2 The title page carries the title, the subtitle if there is one, and the author's name. Nothing else is needed.

P3.3 The copyright page carries the copyright line with the year and the author's name, and “All rights reserved.” A publisher name, an ISBN and a credit line are added only if the book has them.

P3.4 Each front page appears once. A book that already has its own typed title page or copyright page does not get a second one added.

P3.5 The Contents lists every chapter, in order, and every entry leads to its chapter.

P3.6 A credit to the tools you used is your choice. It is never required and never added for you.

# Part 4 · The cover is ready

A cover is seen far more often than it is opened, and almost always small.

P4.1 The front cover is a tall picture, at least 1,600 pixels wide and 2,400 pixels high, saved as a JPEG or PNG.

P4.2 The title and the author's name can be read when the cover is the size of a postage stamp. That is the size a store shows it at.

P4.3 Every picture on the cover is yours to use: you made it, you paid for the right to it, or its license allows it on a book for sale.

P4.4 A light cover has a thin darker edge, so it does not vanish into a white store page.

P4.5 A paperback needs a second, different cover: one wide picture holding the back, the spine and the front together, sized by the printer for your page count. An e-book cover alone will not do for print.

# Part 5 · The e-book file is sound

The e-book is the file most stores ask for. It is called an EPUB.

P5.1 The EPUB passes EPUBCheck, the checker the bookstores themselves use, with no errors.

P5.2 The file carries the book's title, author and language inside it, where a store's computers read them.

P5.3 The file has a working contents list and real chapter headings, so a reader can jump to any chapter.

P5.4 The cover is inside the file and is marked as the cover.

P5.5 The text reflows: a reader can make it bigger or change the typeface, and nothing is cut off. Pictures shrink to fit the screen.

P5.6 The file keeps the same identifier every time the same book is exported, so a store sees an update as the same book and not a new one.

# Part 6 · The print file is sound

A paperback is printed from a PDF. A printer checks it before it prints anything, and sends back a file that does not fit.

P6.1 The page size is one the printer offers. Common sizes for a book of words are 6 × 9, 5.5 × 8.5 and 5 × 8 inches.

P6.2 The inside margin, where the pages are bound, is wide enough for the number of pages. A longer book needs a wider inside margin. The outside margins are at least a quarter inch.

P6.3 Every typeface is embedded in the file, so the printer prints the letters you chose.

P6.4 The file holds the inside pages only. The paperback cover is a separate file.

P6.5 Chapters open on a right-hand page. A blank page carries no number and no header.

P6.6 The book is at least as long as the printer's shortest book. Amazon will not print a paperback shorter than 24 pages.

P6.7 The printed page meets The Inkhorse Formatting Standard, Part 9.

# Part 7 · The details are ready

A store asks for more than the file. Have these written before you sit down to upload.

P7.1 A description of the book, the few paragraphs a shopper reads to decide. Write it, hear it read aloud, and fix it, like any other page.

P7.2 The categories the book belongs in, and a few words a shopper might search for.

P7.3 An ISBN, if the format needs one. An e-book usually does not. A paperback does, and Amazon will give one free for a paperback printed through Amazon.

P7.4 The title, author name and language are typed into the store exactly as they appear in the book, and exactly the same for the e-book and the paperback. Stores join the two into one page only when they match.

P7.5 A price, and a decision about where the book will be sold.

# Part 8 · It has been proofed where it will be read

The book on your screen is not yet the book in the store. Look at it there before a reader does.

P8.1 The exported EPUB has been opened and paged through in a reading app, not only inside Inkhorse.

P8.2 The book has been checked in the store's own previewer before it goes on sale. For a paperback, Amazon requires this step.

P8.3 For a paperback, a printed proof copy has been ordered and read with a pen in hand.

P8.4 The first page of every chapter, the Contents and the last page have each been looked at on their own. Those are the pages where trouble hides.

# Part 9 · The author keeps a copy

Stores change, accounts close, phones break. The book must outlive all three.

P9.1 A backup file of the book is saved outside the app before publishing, and again after every change.

P9.2 The exact files that were uploaded, the EPUB, the PDF and the covers, are kept together in one place, named with the date.

P9.3 A second copy lives somewhere that is not the phone: a computer, a cloud drive, or an email to yourself.

# Part 10 · The stores, in brief

These facts come from each store's own help pages and were checked on November 10, 2026. Stores change their rules. Before you upload, read the store's page yourself; the Sources at the end of this book name them.

## Amazon Kindle (e-book)

Amazon's publishing service is called Kindle Direct Publishing, or KDP. It accepts an EPUB. An ISBN is optional for a Kindle e-book.

The cover is uploaded on its own, as a JPEG or TIFF. Amazon's ideal cover is 2,560 pixels high and 1,600 wide, and it will not take one smaller than 1,000 by 625. A 1,600 by 2,400 cover is well inside those limits, though a little wider in shape than Amazon's ideal.

Amazon recommends checking an EPUB in its free Kindle Previewer before you upload it.

## Amazon paperback

The inside of the book is uploaded as a PDF. Amazon prints paperbacks from 24 to 828 pages.

The inside margin must be at least 0.375 inch for 24 to 150 pages, 0.5 inch for 151 to 300, 0.625 inch for 301 to 500, 0.75 inch for 501 to 700, and 0.875 inch for 701 to 828. The outside margins must be at least 0.25 inch.

A paperback must have an ISBN. Amazon offers a free one, which can be used only on Amazon. A paperback and an e-book never share an ISBN.

The cover is one PDF with the back, spine and front together. Amazon has a calculator that gives its exact size from your page count and paper. Words on the spine are allowed only on a book of at least 79 pages. If your cover has no barcode, Amazon adds one to the back.

Amazon makes you look through the book in its previewer before a paperback can be published, and again after every update. You can also order a printed proof before the book goes on sale.

## Apple Books

Apple Books accepts an EPUB, and the EPUB must pass EPUBCheck. The cover must be a JPEG or PNG at least 1,400 pixels on its shorter side.

## Kobo

Kobo's service is called Kobo Writing Life. It accepts an EPUB. An ISBN is optional: if you have none, Kobo assigns a number of its own. The cover is a JPEG or PNG no larger than 5 megabytes.

## Other routes

Some services take one EPUB and send it to many stores at once, for a share of each sale. That saves work and costs a little of every sale. Read their terms before you choose.

# Part 11 · Where Inkhorse stands today

## P11 · Scorecard, November 12, 2026

An automatic test checks each line of the first list before every change goes out, by opening the real EPUB and print PDF that Inkhorse exports. The two lines marked "no test of its own yet" are the exceptions.

What Inkhorse does for you:

- P2.1 Read aloud reads the whole book to you, and you can fix a word right on the page where you heard it. Both features have tests, but no test of its own yet follows a whole book from start to finish.
- P1.3 Find searches the chapter you are in, and the Book Hub lists every highlight and note in the whole book so none is left behind. No test of its own yet.
- P3.1 to P3.5 Inkhorse builds the title page, copyright page, dedication and Contents from what you type in Book settings, in the right order, each one once. A book with its own typed front pages keeps them and gets no second copy.
- P3.6 The credit line is blank unless you fill it in.
- P4.1 Every cover in Inkhorse is shown at the same tall 2:3 shape, and the whole cover is always shown.
- P5.1 to P5.6 The EPUB that Inkhorse exports carries the title, author and language, a working contents list, real chapter headings, the cover, and an identifier that stays the same for the same book. An exported book is run through EPUBCheck before every change goes out and must pass with no errors and no warnings.
- P6.1 The print PDF comes in 6 × 9, 5.5 × 8.5 and 5 × 8 inches, and 8.5 × 11.
- P6.2 The print PDF uses an inside margin of 0.875 inch, wide enough for a book of any length Amazon prints, and an outside margin of 0.625 inch.
- P6.3 and P6.4 The print PDF has its typeface built into the file and holds no cover.
- P6.5 In the print PDF, chapters open on a right-hand page, and blank pages carry no number and no header.
- P9.1 Save backup file, in the Chapters panel, saves the whole book outside the app.

What is yours to check:

- P1.1, P1.2, P1.4 and P1.5 Inkhorse does not yet warn about a missing author name, an empty chapter or a name spelled two ways.
- P2.2 to P2.4 Only you can decide the book has been heard, fixed and read by someone else.
- P4.2 to P4.4 Inkhorse shows your cover; it does not judge whether the title can be read small, or whether the picture is yours to use.
- P5.2 The language written into the EPUB is the language your device is set to. If you write in another language, check it in the store's listing.
- P6.6 Inkhorse does not stop you from exporting a print PDF shorter than 24 pages.
- All of Part 7 and Part 8. The description, the categories, the price, the store's previewer and the printed proof all happen outside Inkhorse.
- P9.2 and P9.3 Inkhorse saves the backup file; where you keep it is up to you.

Not yet:

- P4.5 Inkhorse does not make the wide paperback cover with a spine. Amazon's own cover tools can.
- P6.7 The print PDF meets the Formatting Standard's print rules, except the ones Inkhorse does not do anywhere yet: footnotes, and lists or quoted passages. Part 11 of The Inkhorse Formatting Standard has the full list. Read a print proof with care.
- P1.3 and P1.5 Find works one chapter at a time. Inkhorse cannot yet search the whole book at once.
- P7.1 Inkhorse has no place to write and keep the store description.
- A single “Ready to publish?” check that runs a book against this standard and shows a tick or a note for every rule. This book comes first; the check is meant to follow.

# How we keep the standard

1. A change to how Inkhorse exports a book names the rules it touches, here and in The Inkhorse Formatting Standard.
2. Each rule a machine can check gets an automatic test.
3. Every change goes to a preview page first. It goes live only after the full automatic check passes and Steve Patient, who makes Inkhorse, approves it on his own phone.
4. The scorecard in Part 11 is kept true: when a rule's standing changes, the scorecard changes with it, and its heading says the date.
5. The store facts in Part 10 are checked again against the stores' own pages whenever this book is updated, and Part 10 says the date.
